\section{Purpose}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------

This note records the chosen thermal-hydraulic architecture and the
first-pass sizing calculations that support it.  It does three things:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item documents the operating envelope and loop topology that have been
        selected for the next implementation,
  \item derives the reactor-scale flow, heat-removal, and pump targets that
        drive the corresponding Modelica design.
\end{enumerate}

The note is intentionally close to the engineering-design level, not a
safety case.  Its output is a concrete Modelica subsystem breakdown ready
for the next coding pass.

\section{Reactor-scale inputs}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------

The frozen geometry and power level from the repository are repeated here
for reference:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lc}
\hline
Quantity & Value \\
\hline
Nominal thermal power $P$ & $20\ \mathrm{MW}$ \\
Inner moderator radius $R_{\mathrm{inner}}$ & $0.80\ \mathrm{m}$ \\
Fuel outer radius $R_{\mathrm{fuel}}$ & $3.456\ \mathrm{m}$ \\
Active height $H$ & $6.912\ \mathrm{m}$ \\
Primary coolant & D$_2$O \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The active annular volume and average power density follow directly:
\[
V_{\mathrm{core}}
= \pi\!\left(R_{\mathrm{fuel}}^2 - R_{\mathrm{inner}}^2\right) H
\approx 245.5\ \mathrm{m^3},
\qquad
q''' = \frac{P}{V_{\mathrm{core}}} \approx 81.5\ \mathrm{kW/m^3}.
\]

This power density is low for a research reactor, which is consistent with
the very large, heavily moderated annular geometry.  The cooling challenge
is therefore one of \emph{flow capacity}, not heat-flux intensity.

\section{Selected operating envelope}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------

The following primary-loop temperatures and bulk temperature rise have
been selected for the next Modelica implementation:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lc}
\hline
Quantity & Selected value \\
\hline
Primary inlet bulk temperature $T_{\mathrm{in}}$ & $25^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ \\
Nominal outlet bulk temperature $T_{\mathrm{out,nom}}$ & $45^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ \\
Upper-bound outlet temperature $T_{\mathrm{out,max}}$ & $55^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ \\
Nominal bulk temperature rise $\Delta T_{\mathrm{nom}}$ & $20\ \mathrm{K}$ \\
Upper-bound bulk temperature rise $\Delta T_{\mathrm{max}}$ & $30\ \mathrm{K}$ \\
Primary coolant flow direction & top-to-bottom \\
Primary pressure regime & low-pressure ($\lesssim 3\ \mathrm{bar}$ gauge) \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The inlet temperature of $25^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ is set by the return
temperature from the pool and the secondary heat exchanger.  The nominal
outlet of $45^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ keeps the entire primary loop
comfortably below saturation at any plausible low-gauge operating
pressure and well within the single-phase D$_2$O regime.  The $55^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$
upper bound is a conservative operating limit, not a safety margin; the
actual saturation temperature at 3 bar gauge is approximately
$133^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$.

\section{Reactor-scale flow and heat-removal estimates}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Required primary mass flow}

Using D$_2$O properties at the operating temperature midpoint
($\approx 35^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$):
\[
c_p \approx 4215\ \mathrm{J/(kg\,K)},
\qquad
\rho \approx 1100\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}.
\]

The energy balance gives:
\[
\dot{m} = \frac{P}{c_p\,\Delta T}.
\]

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\hline
Case & $\Delta T$ & $\dot{m}$ [kg/s] & $\dot{V}$ [m$^3$/h] \\
\hline
Nominal design point & 20 K & 237 & 776 \\
Upper-bound design point & 30 K & 158 & 518 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The nominal design flow of approximately $237\ \mathrm{kg/s}$
($776\ \mathrm{m^3/h}$) is the primary target for pump and piping sizing.
The upper-bound flow of $158\ \mathrm{kg/s}$ sets the lower flow limit
at which the outlet temperature constraint is still met.

\subsection{Pump duty}

The pump shaft power scales with the loop pressure drop:
\[
W_{\mathrm{pump}} \approx \frac{\Delta p \cdot \dot{V}}{\eta}, \qquad \eta \approx 0.7.
\]

For the nominal design point:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\hline
Loop $\Delta p$ & $W_{\mathrm{pump}}$ (nominal $\dot{m}$) & $W_{\mathrm{pump}}$ (upper-bound $\dot{m}$) \\
\hline
1 bar & $31\ \mathrm{kW}$ & $21\ \mathrm{kW}$ \\
2 bar & $62\ \mathrm{kW}$ & $41\ \mathrm{kW}$ \\
3 bar & $92\ \mathrm{kW}$ & $62\ \mathrm{kW}$ \\
5 bar & $154\ \mathrm{kW}$ & $103\ \mathrm{kW}$ \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

A total loop pressure drop in the range of 2--3 bar is a plausible first
estimate for a large-scale research-reactor primary circuit, giving a pump
duty on the order of 60--90 kW at the nominal flow.

\subsection{Pool thermal time constant}

The surrounding pool (Section~\ref{sec:pool}) has a volume of
$2535\ \mathrm{m^3}$ ($2.535 \times 10^6\ \mathrm{litres}$).  Its
thermal time constant at full reactor power with no active cooling is:
\[
\tau = \frac{m_{\mathrm{pool}}\,c_{p,\mathrm{water}}}{P}
= \frac{2535 \times 10^3 \times 4200}{20 \times 10^6}
\approx 530\ \mathrm{s}
\approx 9\ \mathrm{min}.
\]

This confirms that the pool cannot serve as the primary heat sink for
sustained operation: at full power it would heat up by 20 K in roughly
9 minutes.  The primary forced-circulation loop is therefore the
essential heat-removal path, and the pool is a structural heat reservoir
for short transients only.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Core, reflector, and pool abstraction}
\label{sec:pool}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Actively cooled region}

Forced primary D$_2$O flow is restricted to the fuel annulus and the
inner moderator cylinder, i.e.\ the region from $r = 0$ to
$r = R_{\mathrm{fuel}} = 3.456\ \mathrm{m}$.  The outer D$_2$O reflector
($R_{\mathrm{fuel}} < r \leq R_{\mathrm{refl}} = 4.056\ \mathrm{m}$) is
not part of the forced-circulation path.

The detailed distribution of flow among individual fuel elements, coolant
tubes, or sub-channels inside the active region is \emph{not modelled}.
Instead, the entire active region is represented by one
\textbf{effective coolant channel} with:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the total primary mass flow passing through it,
  \item the axially distributed core power profile as its heat input,
  \item an effective flow cross-section and hydraulic diameter chosen to
        give a representative pressure drop.
\end{itemize}

This single-channel abstraction is parameterised so that later refinement
to multiple parallel channels (sharing inlet and outlet plena) can be
done without restructuring the rest of the loop.

\subsection{Flow direction}

The primary coolant flows \textbf{top to bottom} through the effective
core channel.  The inlet is at the top of the active region; the outlet
is at the bottom.  This matches the natural downward-flow convention seen
in several research-reactor designs and means the hottest bulk coolant
leaves at the bottom before routing to the heat exchanger.

\subsection{Stagnant reflector and pool}

The outer D$_2$O reflector and the surrounding pool water together
constitute a large stagnant inventory that provides:
\begin{itemize}
  \item long-term thermal mass for decay-heat transient absorption,
  \item shielding of personnel and instrumentation,
  \item neutron moderation in the outer reflector zone.
\end{itemize}

The rectangular pool surrounding the reactor vessel has approximate
dimensions of $15\ \mathrm{m}\ (\mathrm{height}) \times
13\ \mathrm{m} \times 13\ \mathrm{m}\ (\mathrm{base})$, giving a total
volume of approximately $2535\ \mathrm{m^3}$ ($2.535 \times 10^6$
litres).

In the next Modelica model, this inventory is represented as a
\textbf{fixed-temperature or slowly varying thermal boundary condition}.
Active pool cooling, pool-loop flow, and pool temperature dynamics are
not modelled in the first implementation pass.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Simplified primary loop}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------

The primary D$_2$O loop follows a fixed topology inspired by
forced-circulation research reactors such as FRM~II, but simplified
aggressively to the minimum useful set of components.

\subsection{Topology}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Upper inlet plenum.}  A vessel volume at the top of the
        core that distributes incoming primary coolant across the
        effective core channel entrance and provides hydraulic
        decoupling.
  \item \textbf{Effective core channel.}  A single axially discretised
        heated pipe (8 nodes) carrying the total primary flow top to
        bottom.  Heat input follows the axial power profile from the
        neutronics model.
  \item \textbf{Lower outlet plenum.}  A vessel volume at the bottom of
        the core that collects the heated coolant before it exits to the
        primary piping.
  \item \textbf{Primary pump.}  A single centrifugal pump model with a
        representative head curve operating on the D$_2$O primary
        inventory.  Nominal operating point: $\approx 237\ \mathrm{kg/s}$
        at $2$--$3\ \mathrm{bar}$ head.
  \item \textbf{Primary heat exchanger (primary side).}  A counterflow
        shell-and-tube or plate-heat-exchanger model on the primary side
        that removes the full 20 MW to the secondary loop at nominal
        conditions.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Selected nominal operating parameters}

\begin{center}

Inlet temperature & $25^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ \\

\section{Simplified secondary loop}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------

The secondary side removes heat from the primary loop and rejects it to
an ultimate heat sink.  It is modelled at the minimum useful resolution:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Secondary heat exchanger (secondary side).}  The
        secondary-side complement of the primary HX above; coupled
        thermally via a fixed or parameterised overall heat-transfer
        coefficient $UA$.
  \item \textbf{Secondary pump.}  A single pump circulating light-water
        ($\mathrm{H_2O}$) through the secondary side.
  \item \textbf{Feed tank / source boundary.}  A cold-water source at
        approximately $20^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ representing the secondary
        supply.
  \item \textbf{Sink tank / discharge boundary.}  A downstream boundary
        that receives warmed secondary water and represents the ultimate
        heat sink without modelling it in detail.
\end{enumerate}

There is no tertiary loop in this model.  The secondary side is not
intended to represent a real balance-of-plant; it exists to close the
energy balance and allow transient study of primary-to-secondary
coupling.

\subsection{Key scaling parameters}

The following parameters must be set to reactor scale, not to laboratory
scale, in the next model:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Nominal power:} $20\ \mathrm{MW}$ (currently prototype uses $\sim 10\ \mathrm{kW}$).
  \item \textbf{Primary nominal flow:} $237\ \mathrm{kg/s}$.
  \item \textbf{Pipe diameter and cross-section:} sized so that the
        channel velocity and pressure drop are physically plausible at
        the nominal flow (not the current $\sim 10\ \mathrm{mm}$ toy pipe).
  \item \textbf{HX heat-transfer area:} sized to remove 20 MW with a
        primary-to-secondary temperature approach of $\approx 10$--$15\ \mathrm{K}$.
\end{itemize}

\section{What is deliberately out of scope}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------

The following items are excluded from the first implementation pass:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Multiple parallel primary pumps or parallel heat-exchanger trains.
  \item Detailed in-core flow distribution or hot-channel analysis.
  \item Outer reflector cooling flow.
  \item Active pool cooling loop.
  \item Pool temperature dynamics (pool treated as a fixed boundary).
  \item Tertiary heat-rejection loop.
  \item Two-phase flow or void fraction modelling.
  \item Decay-heat tracking after shutdown.
  \item Safety system actuation or emergency core cooling.
  \item Full balance-of-plant or turbine cycle.
\end{itemize}

These items are noted here so that the implementation boundary is
explicit and so that future extensions can be planned against this
baseline rather than discovered incrementally.
